# 💧 awqinc-com-documentation

A friendly little toolkit that grabs every water-treatment equipment manual from [Air & Water Quality's manuals page](https://awqinc.com/water-treatment-equipment-manuals/) and saves them locally in a `PDFs/` folder — building a handy, offline library anyone can learn from. 📚✨

Clean water touches almost everything: your health, your appliances, your utility bills, your business's equipment, and entire cities' infrastructure. This repo exists so that knowledge doesn't stay locked inside one company's website — it becomes a downloadable, shareable, teachable resource for anyone, anywhere. 🌍

## 🌟 Why these systems matter — the real benefits, with real numbers

Hard water and untreated water aren't just cosmetic annoyances — they cost real money and cause real wear. Here's the data behind why these systems exist:

- **Water hardness scale** (US Geological Survey): Soft water is 0–60 mg/L (0–3.5 grains per gallon, "gpg"), moderately hard is 60–120 mg/L (3.5–7 gpg), hard is 120–180 mg/L (7–10.5 gpg), and very hard is anything above 180 mg/L (10.5+ gpg). The median US city sits around **100 mg/L (≈5.8 gpg) — moderately hard** — but plenty of regions (much of the Southwest and Midwest, in particular) sit well into the "hard" or "very hard" range. 📊
- **Scale kills efficiency.** Mineral scale buildup inside a water heater can insulate the heating element enough to cut its efficiency by roughly **25–40%**, and can shorten a heater's working life from 12–15 years down to as little as 6–8 years in hard-water areas. A softener directly prevents this. 🔥
- **Hardness ≠ health risk, but it is a wallet risk.** The EPA sets no health-based limit for calcium/magnesium — hard water is safe to drink and even nutritionally slightly beneficial. What it _does_ do is cause scale, dull soap lathering, spotted dishes, and dry skin/hair — all quality-of-life and cost-of-ownership issues that softeners solve. 💰
- **Reverse osmosis is extremely effective.** RO membranes can reject the large majority of dissolved solids and many other contaminants, and combined with proper treatment can help systems remove up to **99% of most radionuclide contaminants** according to EPA-referenced treatment standards. That's why RO shows up at both the kitchen-tap scale (the Epro unit in these manuals) and in industrial-scale municipal plants. 🧬
- **Radon in water is a real, documented risk.** The EPA's action level for radon in **air** is **4 pCi/L**, and while there's no finalized federal standard specifically for radon in water, EPA has proposed levels around **300–4,000 pCi/L** depending on state programs, and industry groups (AARST) recommend mitigation starting around **4,000 pCi/L** in water. As a rule of thumb, every **10,000 pCi/L of radon in water** adds roughly **1 pCi/L to indoor air** as it's released during showering, washing, and boiling — which is why well owners in radon-prone regions (like Maine, where this company operates) take it seriously. ☢️➡️🌬️
- **Chemical feed accuracy matters.** Metering pumps like the Pulsatron and Chemtech units dose chemicals (chlorine, anti-scalants, pH adjusters) in tiny, precise, continuous amounts — the same core principle used at municipal treatment plants, just at a much smaller scale.

1. **Pick the right softener or filter** — compare specs before you buy, so you get a unit sized right for your home's hardness (in gpg) and daily water usage. 🛒

3. **DIY maintenance** — salt refills, resin cleaning, filter swaps — save money by doing simple upkeep yourself. 🧹
4. **Fix common problems** — the Fleck service manual covers fault codes, so low pressure doesn't always mean a new unit. 🩹

6. **Keep a maintenance log** — protect your warranty by tracking filter changes the way manufacturers expect. 📝
7. **Know your warranty** — understand exactly what's covered before something breaks, not after. 🛡️
8. **Radon-safe well water** — the CleaRadon manual helps well owners in radon-prone areas reduce a real, measurable health risk. 🌬️

### 🏢 Small business (restaurants, laundromats, offices, clinics, breweries)

10. **Dial in chemical dosing** — the Pulsatron/Chemtech manuals cover pump calibration for chlorine, anti-scalant, or pH adjustment. ⚗️
11. **Build a real PM schedule** — combine the service manual and maintenance log into a proper preventive-maintenance calendar. 📅
12. **Train new staff fast** — hand new hires the manual instead of relying only on word-of-mouth instructions. 🎓

16. **Public education** — city water departments can point curious residents to plain-language manuals like these to explain "why does my water taste different?" 💬
17. **Smarter procurement** — reading real manuals helps non-engineers ask sharper questions when evaluating vendor bids. 📋

## 🏗️ How to use AWQ's actual systems, and install one at home or business

If you want to go beyond documentation and get a real system running:

1. **Test your water first** — get a hardness reading (in gpg or ppm), and test for iron, radon, and total dissolved solids (TDS) if you're on a private well. This single step determines everything else. 🧫
2. **Match the problem to the equipment** —
   - Hardness above ~7 gpg (120 mg/L)? → a softener (S250, TQ, 2060, K5, or Powerline). 🌀
   - Want lower TDS / better-tasting drinking water? → an RO system (Epro 1500). 🥤
   - Need continuous chemical treatment (disinfection, anti-scale, pH correction)? → a metering pump (Pulsatron/Chemtech). ⚗️
   - Radon detected in well water? → a CleaRadon unit. ☢️
3. **Pick your exact model** — use the manuals in this repo to compare capacity (grains, gallons per day, dosing rate) against your household or business's actual usage. 🔍
4. **Get it installed** — [Air & Water Quality](https://awqinc.com/) installs and services this exact equipment for customers in Maine, or you can self-install using the owner's manual if you're comfortable with basic plumbing. 🧰
5. **Set it up correctly** — follow the manual's plumbing diagram, bypass valve placement, and initial startup/regeneration steps closely — this is where most first-time mistakes happen. ⚙️
6. **Maintain it** — use the RO Maintenance and Log manual as a template to track filter and membrane changes going forward, and keep it for warranty purposes. 🗓️
7. **Need help?** — Air & Water Quality can be reached directly at **800-698-9655** for anything not covered here, from missing manuals to full installs. ☎️

## ⚠️ Honest limitations

These are manufacturer manuals for specific residential/light-commercial products — not municipal engineering codes and not a replacement for a licensed engineer on anything serving more than a household or small facility. The underlying principles scale up beautifully for learning; the design authority for public water systems does not. Always defer to local drinking-water regulations and a licensed professional for anything bigger than "my house" or "my small business." Numbers above (hardness ranges, radon levels, efficiency losses) come from USGS/EPA-referenced sources and general industry data — always check your own local water report and a certified test for anything you're making a real decision on. 🙏
